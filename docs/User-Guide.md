# User guide — what to expect before, during and after

Written for end users. Everything here is generic — adapt names and dates per customer.

## Before cutover

- You receive a **new PC**. It signs you in to the new environment.
- You may sign in on the new PC ahead of time to register MFA (your old MFA registration
  does not move — it must be set up in the new environment; a Temporary Access Pass may
  be provided by the help desk) — but **do not open Outlook,
  OneDrive or Teams on it yet**: your data isn't there until the cutover finishes.
- Keep using the old PC as normal until cutover weekend.
- Files you keep **outside** OneDrive (Desktop if not synced, local folders, PST files) do not
  move — check your old PC before it's wiped, and tell IT about anything stored locally.
- Your old mail, calendar, contacts, OneDrive files and Teams chats move automatically. Some
  things genuinely don't (see the "what changes" list below).

## During the cutover weekend

- The old environment stops answering on <date/time>. Don't send mail from it after then.
- Leave the old PC switched on until told otherwise — it's the reference if something's missing.

## After cutover (Monday morning)

Follow **[After-Cutover-User-Guide.md](After-Cutover-User-Guide.md)** — a numbered day-1 and
first-week checklist (new Outlook profile, re-accept meetings, mobile accounts, shared
mailboxes, what to check). In short:

- Sign in on the **new PC** with your new account. MFA may ask again — that's expected.
- **Create a new Outlook profile** — don't reuse the old one; it points at the old tenant.
- **Teams**: sign in fresh and **re-accept your meetings** — RSVPs were reset.

## What doesn't come across (ask IT, not the help desk)

- Teams channel structure/chat is only as complete as the pilot proved.
- Custom dictionary, Outlook signatures and other per-profile settings.
- Printer mappings, VPN profiles — installed fresh on the new PC.

## If something's wrong

Contact <help desk>. Tell them your old PC is untouched — almost everything can be re-checked
against it while it still exists.
